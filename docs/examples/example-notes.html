Introduction

A body performs simple harmonic motion (SHM) when the restoring force on it is proportional to its displacement from the mean position and always directed towards it.

Simple harmonic motion is periodic motion in which the acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement from the mean position and is always directed towards that position.

\[ a = -\omega^2 x \] where \(a\) = acceleration, \(\omega\) = angular frequency, \(x\) = displacement from the mean position.

Displacement, velocity and acceleration

Displacement

The displacement of a particle in SHM at time \(t\) is \(x = A\sin(\omega t + \phi)\), where \(A\) is the amplitude and \(\phi\) is the initial phase.

Velocity

\[ v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} \]

Velocity is maximum (\(v_{max} = \omega A\)) at the mean position and zero at the extreme positions.

PositionDisplacementVelocityAcceleration
Mean position0\(\omega A\) (maximum)0
Extreme position\(\pm A\)0\(\omega^2 A\) (maximum)

Energy in SHM

Kinetic energy

\(K = \tfrac12 m\omega^2 (A^2 - x^2)\)

Potential energy

\(U = \tfrac12 m\omega^2 x^2\)

The total energy \(E = K + U = \tfrac12 m\omega^2 A^2\) stays constant throughout the motion.

Worked example

A particle executes SHM with amplitude 5 cm and period 2 s. Find its maximum speed.

Show solution

\(\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} = \dfrac{2\pi}{2} = \pi\ \text{rad s}^{-1}\)

\(v_{max} = \omega A = \pi \times 0.05 = 0.157\ \text{m s}^{-1}\)

In the exam, always convert the amplitude to metres before substituting.

Do not confuse angular frequency \(\omega\) (rad/s) with frequency \(f\) (Hz): \(\omega = 2\pi f\).

Key points

  • Restoring force \(F = -kx\).
  • Time period \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}\) for a mass–spring system.
  • Total energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude.
