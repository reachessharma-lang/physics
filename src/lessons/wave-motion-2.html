6.1 Progressive Wave

Wave motion: Wave motion is the propagation of a disturbance through a medium or space from one point to another without the actual transfer of matter as a whole.

Progressive wave: A progressive wave is a wave in which the disturbance travels continuously through the medium from one point to another, transferring energy from one region to another without permanent displacement of the particles of the medium.

Characteristics of progressive waves

  • The wave travels continuously through the medium.
  • Energy is transferred from one point to another.
  • Particles of the medium vibrate about their mean positions.
  • Different particles generally have different phases of vibration.
  • All particles have the same frequency as the source.
  • For an ideal progressive wave, all particles have the same amplitude.
  • The displacement of particles varies with both position and time.
  • There is no fixed node or antinode.
  • The wave velocity depends mainly on the properties of the medium.

Important wave quantities

Quantity Symbol SI unit Meaning
Amplitude \(A\) m Maximum displacement from mean position
Wavelength \(\lambda\) m Distance between two successive particles in the same phase
Frequency \(f\) Hz Number of vibrations per second
Time period \(T\) s Time required for one complete vibration
Angular frequency \(\omega\) rad s\(^{-1}\) Rate of change of phase with time
Wave number \(k\) rad m\(^{-1}\) Rate of change of phase with distance
Wave velocity \(v\) m s\(^{-1}\) Speed with which the disturbance propagates
\[ f=\frac{1}{T} \]
\[ \omega=2\pi f=\frac{2\pi}{T} \]\(\omega\) is angular frequency.
\[ k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \]\(k\) is the wave number.
\[ \boxed{v=f\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{T}} \]\(v\) is wave velocity.

6.2 Mathematical Form of a Progressive Wave

Consider a particle at the origin executing simple harmonic motion:

\[ y=A\sin\omega t \]\(A\) is amplitude and \(\omega\) is angular frequency.

Consider another particle at a distance \(x\) from the origin. The disturbance takes a time \(x/v\) to reach it.

Therefore, its displacement at time \(t\) is

\[ y=A\sin\left[\omega\left(t-\frac{x}{v}\right)\right] \]

Since

\[ v=f\lambda=\frac{\omega}{k} \]

we have

\[ \frac{\omega}{v}=k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \]

Hence the equation of a progressive wave travelling in the positive \(x\)-direction is

\[ \boxed{y=A\sin(\omega t-kx)} \]\(A\) is amplitude, \(\omega\) is angular frequency, \(k=2\pi/\lambda\) is wave number, \(x\) is position and \(t\) is time.

Equivalent forms are

\[ \boxed{y=A\sin 2\pi\left(\frac{t}{T}-\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)} \]
and

\[ \boxed{y=A\sin(\omega t-kx)} \]

Wave travelling in the negative \(x\)-direction

If the wave travels in the negative \(x\)-direction, the equation becomes

\[ \boxed{y=A\sin(\omega t+kx)} \]

Remember:

  • \(y=A\sin(\omega t-kx)\) represents a wave travelling in the \(+x\)-direction.
  • \(y=A\sin(\omega t+kx)\) represents a wave travelling in the \(-x\)-direction.

Phase of a progressive wave

The phase of the wave is

\[ \phi=\omega t-kx \]

For two particles separated by distance \(\Delta x\) at the same instant, their phase difference is

\[ \boxed{\Delta\phi=k\Delta x =\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x} \]

Thus:

  • If \(\Delta x=\lambda\), phase difference \(=2\pi\).
  • If \(\Delta x=\lambda/2\), phase difference \(=\pi\).
  • If \(\Delta x=\lambda/4\), phase difference \(=\pi/2\).

Particle velocity in a progressive wave

For

\[ y=A\sin(\omega t-kx) \]

the particle velocity is

\[ u=\frac{\partial y}{\partial t} =A\omega\cos(\omega t-kx) \]

Therefore, maximum particle velocity is

\[ \boxed{u_{\max}=A\omega} \]

Important distinction: Wave velocity \(v\) is the speed of propagation of the disturbance, whereas particle velocity \(u\) is the instantaneous velocity of an individual particle of the medium. They are not generally the same.

Worked Example 1 — Finding wave quantities

Problem: A progressive wave is represented by

\[ y=0.04\sin(20\pi t-5\pi x) \]

where \(x\) and \(y\) are in metres and \(t\) is in seconds. Find its amplitude, frequency, wavelength and velocity.

Show solution

Compare with

\[ y=A\sin(\omega t-kx) \]

Therefore,

\[ A=0.04\,m \]
\[ \omega=20\pi \]

Hence

\[ f=\frac{\omega}{2\pi} =\frac{20\pi}{2\pi} =10\,Hz \]

Also,

\[ k=5\pi=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \]

Therefore,

\[ \lambda=\frac{2\pi}{5\pi}=0.4\,m \]

Finally,

\[ v=f\lambda=(10)(0.4)=4\,m\,s^{-1} \]

Answer: \(A=0.04\,m,\ f=10\,Hz,\ \lambda=0.4\,m,\ v=4\,m\,s^{-1}\).

Worked Example 2 — Time period and wave velocity

Problem: A progressive wave has wavelength \(2\,m\) and frequency \(25\,Hz\). Find its velocity and time period.

Show solution
\[ v=f\lambda=(25)(2)=50\,m\,s^{-1} \]
\[ T=\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{25}=0.04\,s \]

Answer: \(v=50\,m\,s^{-1}\) and \(T=0.04\,s\).

6.3 Formation of Stationary Waves

Stationary wave: A stationary or standing wave is formed when two progressive waves of the same frequency, amplitude and wavelength travel through the same medium in opposite directions and superpose.

Suppose two waves travelling in opposite directions are

\[ y_1=A\sin(\omega t-kx) \]
and

\[ y_2=A\sin(\omega t+kx) \]

By the principle of superposition,

\[ y=y_1+y_2 \]

Using

\[ \sin C+\sin D =2\sin\frac{C+D}{2}\cos\frac{C-D}{2} \]

we obtain

\[ \boxed{y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx} \]

This is the mathematical equation of a stationary wave.

Conditions for formation of stationary waves

  • The two waves must have the same frequency.
  • They must have the same wavelength.
  • They must have equal or nearly equal amplitudes for a clear stationary pattern.
  • They must travel in opposite directions.
  • They must propagate through the same medium.
  • Their superposition must occur continuously.

Characteristics of stationary waves

  • There is no net transfer of energy along the medium.
  • Fixed points of zero displacement are called nodes.
  • Points of maximum displacement are called antinodes.
  • Particles between two consecutive nodes vibrate in phase.
  • Particles in adjacent loops vibrate in opposite phase.
  • The amplitude varies from zero at nodes to maximum at antinodes.
  • All particles between two adjacent nodes have the same frequency.

Node and antinode

Node: A point in a stationary wave where the displacement is always zero is called a node.

Antinode: A point in a stationary wave where the displacement has maximum amplitude is called an antinode.

For a stationary wave, the distance between two consecutive nodes is

\[ \boxed{\frac{\lambda}{2}} \]

The distance between two consecutive antinodes is also

\[ \boxed{\frac{\lambda}{2}} \]

The distance between a node and the nearest antinode is

\[ \boxed{\frac{\lambda}{4}} \]

6.4 Mathematical Form of a Stationary Wave

Consider two progressive waves of equal amplitude \(A\), angular frequency \(\omega\), and wave number \(k\), travelling in opposite directions:

\[ y_1=A\sin(\omega t-kx) \]
\[ y_2=A\sin(\omega t+kx) \]

The resultant displacement is

\[ y=y_1+y_2 \]

Therefore,

\[ y=A\left[\sin(\omega t-kx)+\sin(\omega t+kx)\right] \]

Using the identity

\[ \sin C+\sin D =2\sin\frac{C+D}{2}\cos\frac{C-D}{2} \]

we obtain

\[ \boxed{y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx} \]

Thus, the amplitude at position \(x\) is

\[ \boxed{A_x=2A|\cos kx|} \]

Therefore, the amplitude is not constant throughout the medium.

Equation for nodes

At a node, displacement is always zero. Therefore,

\[ \cos kx=0 \]

Hence

\[ kx=\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} \]

Since \(k=2\pi/\lambda\),

\[ \frac{2\pi x}{\lambda} =\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} \]

Therefore, positions of nodes are

\[ \boxed{x=\frac{(2n+1)\lambda}{4}} \]\(n=0,1,2,3,\ldots\)

Equation for antinodes

At an antinode, the amplitude is maximum:

\[ |\cos kx|=1 \]

Therefore,

\[ kx=n\pi \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{x=\frac{n\lambda}{2}} \]\(n=0,1,2,3,\ldots\)

Amplitude of stationary wave

The resultant amplitude at position \(x\) is

\[ \boxed{A_x=2A|\cos kx|} \]

The maximum amplitude is therefore

\[ \boxed{A_{\max}=2A} \]

and at a node,

\[ \boxed{A_{\min}=0} \]

Unlike a progressive wave, the amplitude of a stationary wave depends upon position.

Particle velocity in a stationary wave

For

\[ y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx \]

the particle velocity is

\[ u=\frac{\partial y}{\partial t} =2A\omega\cos\omega t\cos kx \]

At a node, \(\cos kx=0\), so the particle velocity is always zero.

At an antinode, the particle velocity is maximum.

Worked Example 3 — Position of nodes and antinodes

Problem: A stationary wave is represented by

\[ y=0.08\sin(40\pi t)\cos(2\pi x) \]

where \(x\) and \(y\) are in metres. Find the wavelength and the positions of the first three nodes.

Show solution

Comparing with

\[ y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx \]

we have

\[ k=2\pi \]

But

\[ k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \]

Therefore,

\[ \lambda=1\,m \]

Nodes occur at

\[ x=\frac{(2n+1)\lambda}{4} \]

Thus the first three nodes are

\[ x=\frac14,\quad\frac34,\quad\frac54\ m \]

Answer: Wavelength \(=1\,m\); first three nodes are at \(0.25\,m,\ 0.75\,m,\ 1.25\,m\).

6.5 Numerical Applications of Progressive Waves

Finding frequency

\[ \boxed{f=\frac{v}{\lambda}} \]

Finding wavelength

\[ \boxed{\lambda=\frac{v}{f}} \]

Finding time period

\[ \boxed{T=\frac{1}{f}} \]

Finding angular frequency

\[ \boxed{\omega=2\pi f} \]

Finding wave number

\[ \boxed{k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}} \]

Finding particle displacement

For a wave

\[ y=A\sin(\omega t-kx) \]

substitute the specified \(x\) and \(t\) to calculate the instantaneous displacement.

Worked Example 4 — NEB-style wave equation

Problem: A wave travelling along the positive \(x\)-axis is represented by

\[ y=0.02\sin(100\pi t-4\pi x) \]

Find its amplitude, frequency, wavelength and velocity.

Show solution

Comparing with

\[ y=A\sin(\omega t-kx) \]

we get

\[ A=0.02\,m,\qquad \omega=100\pi,\qquad k=4\pi \]

Therefore,

\[ f=\frac{100\pi}{2\pi}=50\,Hz \]

and

\[ \lambda=\frac{2\pi}{4\pi}=0.5\,m \]

Thus

\[ v=f\lambda=(50)(0.5)=25\,m\,s^{-1} \]

Answer: \(A=0.02\,m,\ f=50\,Hz,\ \lambda=0.5\,m,\ v=25\,m\,s^{-1}\).

Worked Example 5 — Phase difference

Problem: Two particles in a progressive wave are separated by \(0.25\,m\). If the wavelength is \(1\,m\), find their phase difference.

Show solution
\[ \Delta\phi=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x \]
\[ \Delta\phi= \frac{2\pi}{1}(0.25) =\frac{\pi}{2} \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\Delta\phi=90^\circ} \]

Worked Example 6 — Finding displacement

Problem: A progressive wave is given by

\[ y=0.05\sin(20\pi t-2\pi x) \]

Find the displacement of the particle at \(x=0.25\,m\) and \(t=0.05\,s\).

Show solution
\[ y=0.05\sin[20\pi(0.05)-2\pi(0.25)] \]
\[ y=0.05\sin(\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}) \]
\[ y=0.05\sin\frac{\pi}{2} =0.05\,m \]

Answer: \(y=0.05\,m=5\,cm\).

6.6 Derivation of Stationary Wave Using Two Progressive Waves

Consider two identical progressive waves travelling in opposite directions along the same line.

The first wave travels in the positive \(x\)-direction:

\[ \boxed{y_1=A\sin(\omega t-kx)} \]

The second wave travels in the negative \(x\)-direction:

\[ \boxed{y_2=A\sin(\omega t+kx)} \]

By the principle of superposition, the resultant displacement is

\[ y=y_1+y_2 \]

Therefore,

\[ y=A\sin(\omega t-kx)+A\sin(\omega t+kx) \]

Taking \(A\) common,

\[ y=A[\sin(\omega t-kx)+\sin(\omega t+kx)] \]

Using the identity

\[ \sin C+\sin D =2\sin\frac{C+D}{2}\cos\frac{C-D}{2} \]

we obtain

\[ y=2A\sin\left[ \frac{(\omega t-kx)+(\omega t+kx)}{2} \right] \cos\left[ \frac{(\omega t-kx)-(\omega t+kx)}{2} \right] \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx} \]

This is the equation of a stationary wave.

Alternative form

Depending upon the initial phase and choice of origin, a stationary wave can also be written as

\[ \boxed{y=2A\cos\omega t\sin kx} \]

Both forms describe a stationary wave; the apparent difference is due to the choice of time origin and spatial origin.

Derivation of positions of nodes

For the stationary wave

\[ y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx \]

At a node, \(y=0\) for all values of \(t\). Therefore,

\[ \cos kx=0 \]

Thus

\[ kx=\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} \]

Using \(k=2\pi/\lambda\),

\[ \boxed{x=\frac{(2n+1)\lambda}{4}} \]

Derivation of positions of antinodes

For an antinode, the amplitude must be maximum. Hence

\[ |\cos kx|=1 \]

Therefore,

\[ kx=n\pi \]

and hence

\[ \boxed{x=\frac{n\lambda}{2}} \]

Separation between nodes and antinodes

\[ \boxed{\text{Distance between consecutive nodes}=\frac{\lambda}{2}} \]
\[ \boxed{\text{Distance between consecutive antinodes}=\frac{\lambda}{2}} \]
\[ \boxed{\text{Distance between a node and nearest antinode}=\frac{\lambda}{4}} \]

NEB-Style Long and Short Numerical Problems

Numerical 1

A progressive wave is represented by \(y=0.05\sin(40\pi t-8\pi x)\). Find its amplitude, frequency, wavelength and velocity.

Show solution
\[ A=0.05\,m \]
\[ f=\frac{40\pi}{2\pi}=20\,Hz \]
\[ \lambda=\frac{2\pi}{8\pi}=0.25\,m \]
\[ v=f\lambda=(20)(0.25)=5\,m\,s^{-1} \]

Answer: \(A=0.05\,m,\ f=20\,Hz,\ \lambda=0.25\,m,\ v=5\,m\,s^{-1}\).

Numerical 2

A wave has velocity \(340\,m\,s^{-1}\) and frequency \(680\,Hz\). Find its wavelength and time period.

Show solution
\[ \lambda=\frac{v}{f} =\frac{340}{680} =0.5\,m \]
\[ T=\frac{1}{680} =1.47\times10^{-3}\,s \]

Answer: \(\lambda=0.5\,m\), \(T=1.47\times10^{-3}\,s\).

Numerical 3

Two particles of a progressive wave are separated by \(0.5\,m\). If the wavelength is \(2\,m\), determine their phase difference.

Show solution
\[ \Delta\phi =\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x =\frac{2\pi}{2}(0.5) =\frac{\pi}{2} \]

Answer: \(\Delta\phi=90^\circ\).

Numerical 4 — Stationary wave

A stationary wave is represented by

\[ y=0.10\sin(50\pi t)\cos(5\pi x) \]

Find its wavelength, frequency and maximum amplitude.

Show solution

Comparing with

\[ y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx \]

we get

\[ 2A=0.10 \quad\Rightarrow\quad A=0.05\,m \]
\[ \omega=50\pi \]

Therefore,

\[ f=\frac{50\pi}{2\pi}=25\,Hz \]

Also,

\[ k=5\pi=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \]

Hence

\[ \lambda=\frac{2\pi}{5\pi}=0.4\,m \]

The maximum resultant amplitude is

\[ A_{\max}=2A=0.10\,m \]

Answer: \(\lambda=0.4\,m,\ f=25\,Hz,\ A_{\max}=0.10\,m\).

Numerical 5 — Node and antinode positions

A stationary wave has wavelength \(0.8\,m\). Find the distance between consecutive nodes and the distance between a node and the nearest antinode.

Show solution
\[ d_{\mathrm{NN}}=\frac{\lambda}{2} =\frac{0.8}{2} =0.4\,m \]
\[ d_{\mathrm{NA}}=\frac{\lambda}{4} =\frac{0.8}{4} =0.2\,m \]

Answer: Node-to-node distance \(=0.4\,m\); node-to-nearest-antinode distance \(=0.2\,m\).

Progressive Wave vs Stationary Wave

Progressive wave Stationary wave
Travels continuously through the medium. Does not show continuous propagation of the wave pattern.
Transfers energy from one point to another. No net energy is transferred along the medium.
Amplitude is generally the same for all particles in an ideal uniform medium. Amplitude varies from zero at nodes to maximum at antinodes.
There are no fixed nodes or antinodes. Fixed nodes and antinodes are present.
Particles generally have different phases depending on their positions. Particles between consecutive nodes vibrate in phase.
Can be represented by one travelling-wave equation. Is produced by superposition of two oppositely travelling waves.

Common Mistakes in NEB Examinations

  • Do not confuse wave velocity with particle velocity.
  • For a wave travelling in \(+x\)-direction, use \(y=A\sin(\omega t-kx)\).
  • For a wave travelling in \(-x\)-direction, use \(y=A\sin(\omega t+kx)\).
  • Remember \(v=f\lambda\), not \(v=f/\lambda\).
  • Remember \(\omega=2\pi f\) and \(k=2\pi/\lambda\).
  • For a stationary wave, the resultant amplitude is \(2A|\cos kx|\) for the chosen form.
  • Do not state that a stationary wave transfers energy continuously from one end to the other.
  • The distance between consecutive nodes is \(\lambda/2\), not \(\lambda\).
  • The distance between a node and the nearest antinode is \(\lambda/4\).
  • At a node, displacement and particle velocity remain zero.
  • Do not confuse the amplitude \(A\) of each constituent progressive wave with the maximum amplitude \(2A\) of the resultant stationary wave.

Key Points for Revision

  • A progressive wave transfers energy from one place to another.
  • The basic equation of a progressive wave travelling in the \(+x\)-direction is \(y=A\sin(\omega t-kx)\).
  • The wave travelling in the \(-x\)-direction is represented by \(y=A\sin(\omega t+kx)\).
  • The fundamental wave relation is \(v=f\lambda\).
  • \(\omega=2\pi f\) and \(k=2\pi/\lambda\).
  • Particle velocity and wave velocity are different physical quantities.
  • A stationary wave is formed by superposition of two identical waves travelling in opposite directions.
  • The equation \(y=2A\sin\omega t\cos kx\) represents a stationary wave.
  • Nodes are points of zero amplitude.
  • Antinodes are points of maximum amplitude.
  • Distance between consecutive nodes is \(\lambda/2\).
  • Distance between consecutive antinodes is \(\lambda/2\).
  • Distance between a node and the nearest antinode is \(\lambda/4\).
  • The maximum amplitude of a stationary wave is \(2A\), where \(A\) is the amplitude of either constituent wave.
  • There is no net energy transfer along an ideal stationary wave.
