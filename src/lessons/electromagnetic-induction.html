Faraday’s Laws of Electromagnetic Induction

Electromagnetic Induction is the phenomenon of generating an electromotive force (emf) and consequently an electric current in a closed circuit by changing the magnetic flux linked with that circuit.

The magnetic flux (\(\Phi\)) through a surface of area \(A\) placed in a uniform magnetic field \(B\) is defined as the total number of magnetic field lines passing normally through that area:

\[\Phi = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A} = B A \cos\theta\]

Where \(\theta\) is the angle between the magnetic field vector \(\vec{B}\) and the normal area vector \(\vec{A}\). SI Unit: Weber (\(\text{Wb}\)) or \(\text{Tesla}\cdot\text{m}^2\). Dimensions: \([M L^2 T^{-2} A^{-1}]\).

For a coil with \(N\) turns, the total magnetic flux linkage is given by \(N\Phi = N B A \cos\theta\).

Statements of Faraday's Laws

Faraday's First Law (Qualitative)

Whenever there is a change in the magnetic flux linked with a closed circuit, an induced electromotive force (emf) is produced in the circuit. The induced emf lasts only as long as the change in magnetic flux continues.

Faraday's Second Law (Quantitative)

The magnitude of the induced emf in a circuit is directly proportional to the time rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the circuit.

\[e \propto \frac{d\Phi}{dt} \implies e = -N \frac{d\Phi}{dt}\]

Where \(N\) is the number of turns in the coil, and the negative sign represents opposition according to Lenz's Law.

Factors Influencing the Magnitude of Induced EMF:

  • Number of turns (\(N\)): \(e \propto N\). Increasing turns increases total flux linkage.
  • Speed of relative motion (\(v\)): Faster movement causes a higher rate of flux change \(\frac{d\Phi}{dt}\).
  • Strength of magnetic field (\(B\)): Stronger magnets create higher flux density.
  • Area of the coil (\(A\)): Larger area encompasses more field lines.

Lenz’s Law and Conservation of Energy

"The direction of any induced current is always such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it."

Proof of Lenz's Law as Conservation of Energy

Consider a bar magnet approaching a closed solenoid with its North pole facing the coil:

  1. As the N-pole approaches, magnetic flux through the coil increases.
  2. According to Lenz's law, the induced current flows in a direction such that the front face of the coil becomes a North pole.
  3. Like magnetic poles repel. Thus, a repulsive magnetic force opposes the inward motion of the magnet.
  4. To keep moving the magnet forward, an external mechanical work must be performed against this repulsive force.
  5. This mechanical work done by the external agent is transformed into electrical energy in the coil, which eventually dissipates as Joule heat (\(I^2 R t\)).

What if the opposite happened? If the approaching N-pole induced a South pole, it would attract the magnet, accelerating it automatically. This would generate electrical energy continuously without doing external mechanical work, violating the Law of Conservation of Energy. Hence, opposition is mandatory!

Fleming's Right-Hand Rule (Generator Rule)

Used to determine the direction of induced current in a straight conductor moving in a magnetic field:

Forefinger Direction of Magnetic Field (\(\vec{B}\))

Thumb Direction of Motion / Force (\(\vec{v}\))

Middle Finger Direction of Induced Current (\(I\))

Construction and Working of A.C. Generator (Alternator)

An A.C. Generator (or Alternator) is an electrical machine that converts mechanical energy into alternating electrical energy based on the principle of electromagnetic induction.

Main Construction Components

1. Armature Coil (ABCD) Consists of a rectangular coil of many turns of insulated copper wire wound over a soft iron core to concentrate magnetic lines.

2. Field Magnets (N-S) Strong permanent electromagnets with concave pole pieces creating a uniform magnetic field \(B\).

3. Slip Rings (\(R_1, R_2\)) Two hollow metallic rings connected to the ends of the armature coil, rotating synchronously with the coil.

4. Carbon Brushes (\(B_1, B_2\)) Two stationary flexible graphite blocks pressed against the slip rings to tap current to external load \(R\).

Mathematical Derivation of Induced EMF

Let:

  • \(N\) = total number of turns in the rectangular armature coil
  • \(A\) = area of the coil (\(A = \text{length} \times \text{breadth}\))
  • \(B\) = uniform magnetic field intensity
  • \(\omega\) = uniform angular velocity of rotation (\(2\pi f\))

At any instant \(t\), let \(\theta\) be the angle between magnetic field \(\vec{B}\) and normal to the coil plane area vector \(\vec{A}\). Since \(\theta = \omega t\), the magnetic flux linked with a single turn is:

\[\Phi = B A \cos\theta = B A \cos(\omega t)\]

The total flux linkage for \(N\) turns is \(N\Phi = N B A \cos(\omega t)\).

According to Faraday's Law of electromagnetic induction, the induced instantaneous EMF \(e\) is:

\[e = -N \frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -N \frac{d}{dt} \left[ B A \cos(\omega t) \right]\] \[e = -N B A \left( -\omega \sin(\omega t) \right) = N B A \omega \sin(\omega t)\] \[e = e_0 \sin(\omega t)\]

Where \(e_0 = N B A \omega\) represents the Peak (Maximum) EMF produced when \(\sin(\omega t) = 1\).

If \(R\) is the resistance of the circuit, the instantaneous induced current \(I\) is:

\[I = \frac{e}{R} = \frac{e_0}{R} \sin(\omega t) = I_0 \sin(\omega t)\]

Phase Cycle Values:

t=0 (\(\theta=0^\circ\))
e = 0

t=T/4 (\(\theta=90^\circ\))
e = +e₀

t=T/2 (\(\theta=180^\circ\))
e = 0

t=3T/4 (\(\theta=270^\circ\))
e = -e₀

t=T (\(\theta=360^\circ\))
e = 0

Eddy Currents (Foucault Currents)

Eddy currents (or Foucault currents) are loops of electrical current induced within bulk metallic conductors when subjected to a changing magnetic field or when moving through a non-uniform magnetic field.

Useful Applications of Eddy Currents

  • 1. Induction Furnace High-frequency alternating magnetic flux induces massive eddy currents in metals inside the furnace, producing intense heat (\(I^2 R\)) sufficient to melt high-temperature alloys.
  • 2. Electromagnetic Braking in Trains Electromagnets positioned directly above metallic train rails induce eddy currents opposing motion (Lenz's Law), providing smooth, frictionless braking without mechanical wear.
  • 3. Deadbeat Galvanometer The moving coil is wound on a light aluminum frame. Eddy currents induced in the frame quickly damp mechanical oscillations, bringing the needle to rest instantly without swinging.
  • 4. Electric Power Meters Traditional induction-type energy meters use a shiny aluminum disc rotated by torque produced by eddy currents induced from alternating power lines.

Eddy Current Nuisance & How to Minimize It

In electrical transformers, motors, and dynamos, eddy currents cause significant energy loss in the form of heat (\(I^2 R\)), lowering overall efficiency and raising internal operating temperature.

Minimization Method: Iron cores are built using thin laminated soft-iron sheets stacked together, insulated from one another by a layer of non-conducting lacquer or varnish. The laminations are arranged parallel to magnetic field lines to break potential large circular current loops into small, high-resistance paths.

Self-Inductance and Mutual Inductance

1. Self-Inductance (\(L\))

SI Unit: Henry (H)

Self-Inductance is the phenomenon by which an opposing induced electromotive force (back EMF) is produced in a coil when the electric current passing through the same coil changes.

The total magnetic flux linkage \(N\Phi\) through the coil is directly proportional to the current \(I\) flowing through it:

\[N\Phi \propto I \implies N\Phi = L I \implies L = \frac{N\Phi}{I}\]

Where \(L\) is the Coefficient of Self-Inductance. Differentiation with respect to time yields:

\[e = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -L \frac{dI}{dt}\]

Definition of 1 Henry (\(\text{H}\)): Self-inductance of a coil is defined as \(1\text{ Henry}\) if an opposing back EMF of \(1\text{ Volt}\) is induced in it when current changes at a rate of \(1\text{ Ampere per second}\) (\(\frac{dI}{dt} = 1\text{ A/s}\)).

Derivation: Self-Inductance of a Long Solenoid

Consider a solenoid of length \(l\), cross-sectional area \(A\), and total turns \(N\). Let \(n = \frac{N}{l}\) be turns per unit length.

1. Magnetic field inside solenoid due to current \(I\): \(B = \mu_0 n I = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{l}\).

2. Flux through 1 turn: \(\Phi = B A = \frac{\mu_0 N I A}{l}\).

3. Total flux linkage for \(N\) turns: \(N\Phi = N \left( \frac{\mu_0 N I A}{l} \right) = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A I}{l}\).

4. Comparing with \(N\Phi = L I\):

\[L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} = \mu_0 n^2 A l\]

If filled with a core of relative permeability \(\mu_r\): \(L = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r N^2 A}{l}\).

2. Mutual Inductance (\(M\))

SI Unit: Henry (H)

Mutual Inductance is the phenomenon where a changing current in one coil (primary) induces an electromotive force in a neighboring secondary coil.

Flux linkage in secondary coil \(N_2 \Phi_2\) is proportional to current in primary coil \(I_1\):

\[N_2 \Phi_2 \propto I_1 \implies N_2 \Phi_2 = M I_1 \implies M = \frac{N_2 \Phi_2}{I_1}\]

Induced EMF in secondary coil:

\[e_2 = -M \frac{dI_1}{dt}\]

Derivation: Mutual Inductance of Two Coaxial Long Solenoids

Consider two coaxial solenoids \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) of equal length \(l\) and common cross-sectional area \(A\), with total turns \(N_1\) and \(N_2\).

1. Current \(I_1\) in primary \(S_1\) sets up magnetic field: \(B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 I_1}{l}\).

2. Magnetic flux linked with single turn of secondary \(S_2\): \(\Phi_2 = B_1 A = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 I_1 A}{l}\).

3. Total flux linkage with secondary coil \(N_2\): \(N_2 \Phi_2 = N_2 \left( \frac{\mu_0 N_1 I_1 A}{l} \right) = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 A I_1}{l}\).

4. Comparing with \(N_2 \Phi_2 = M I_1\):

\[M = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 A}{l}\]

Coupling Coefficient (\(k\)): \(M = k \sqrt{L_1 L_2}\), where \(0 \le k \le 1\). For ideal tight magnetic coupling, \(k = 1\).

Energy Stored in an Inductor

When current in an inductive circuit increases from zero to a steady value \(I\), work must be done against the self-induced back EMF (\(e = -L \frac{dI}{dt}\)). This work done is stored as magnetic potential energy in the magnetic field surrounding the inductor.

Calculus Derivation of Stored Energy

The rate of doing work against self-induced EMF at any instant \(t\) is:

\[P = \frac{dW}{dt} = |e| \cdot I = \left( L \frac{dI}{dt} \right) I\]

Small work done \(dW\) in a small time interval \(dt\):

\[dW = P \, dt = L I \, dI\]

Total work done \(W\) to build up current from \(0\) to steady value \(I_0\):

\[W = \int_{0}^{I_0} L I \, dI = L \left[ \frac{I^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{I_0} = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2\]

Therefore, the energy stored in the magnetic field of the inductor is:

\[U = \frac{1}{2} L I^2\]

Magnetic Energy Density (\(u_B\))

Energy density is defined as the magnetic energy stored per unit volume (\(V = A l\)) of a long solenoid.

Substituting \(L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l}\) and \(B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{l} \implies I = \frac{B l}{\mu_0 N}\) into the energy formula:

\[U = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} \right) \left( \frac{B l}{\mu_0 N} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{B^2}{\mu_0} (A l)\]

\[u_B = \frac{U}{V} = \frac{U}{A l} = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}\]

Transformer: Principle, Construction and Equations

A Transformer is a static electrical device used to step up (increase) or step down (decrease) alternating voltages without changing the frequency of electrical power.

Working Principle

Works on the principle of Mutual Induction between two insulated coils wound over a common laminated magnetic iron core.

Mathematical Derivation of Transformer Ratio

Let \(N_p\) and \(N_s\) be the number of turns in primary and secondary coils respectively. When alternating voltage \(V_p\) is applied across primary, changing magnetic flux \(\frac{d\Phi}{dt}\) links both coils.

Induced EMF in Primary: \(e_p = -N_p \frac{d\Phi}{dt}\)

Induced EMF in Secondary: \(e_s = -N_s \frac{d\Phi}{dt}\)

For an ideal transformer with zero winding resistance (\(V_p = e_p\) and \(V_s = e_s\)):

\[\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} = K\]

Where \(K\) is called the Transformation Ratio.

  • Step-Up Transformer: \(N_s > N_p \implies K > 1 \implies V_s > V_p\) (Voltage increases, current decreases).
  • Step-Down Transformer: \(N_s < N_p \implies K < 1 \implies V_s < V_p\) (Voltage decreases, current increases).

Ideal Current Relation (100% Efficiency)

\(\text{Input Power } P_p = \text{Output Power } P_s \implies V_p I_p = V_s I_s\)

\[\frac{I_p}{I_s} = \frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} = K\]

Importance of Transformer in Long-Distance Power Transmission

Power generated at hydroelectric stations (e.g., 11 kV) must be transmitted across long distances to cities.

1. Power lost as heat in transmission cables of resistance \(R\) is \(P_{\text{loss}} = I^2 R\).

2. By using a Step-Up Transformer at power plant stations, voltage is elevated to very high levels (e.g., 132 kV or 400 kV). Since \(P = V I\), increasing voltage \(V\) reduces current \(I\) proportionately.

3. Reducing current by a factor of 10 reduces transmission power loss \(I^2 R\) by a factor of \(10^2 = 100\) times!

4. At city substations, Step-Down Transformers reduce high voltage safely down to 220 V for domestic consumption.

Sources of Energy Loss in Practical Transformers

In real-world practical transformers, the output power is always less than input power due to various energy losses (\(\eta < 100\%\)).

1. Copper Loss (\(I^2 R\) Loss)

Heat produced (\(I^2 R\)) in the primary and secondary copper windings due to finite electrical resistance.

Minimization: Use thick, low-resistance copper wires for high-current windings.

2. Eddy Current Loss

Alternating magnetic flux induces eddy currents in the solid iron core, dissipating energy as heat.

Minimization: Use laminated core composed of thin iron sheets insulated by varnish.

3. Hysteresis Loss

Repeated magnetization and demagnetization cycles of the iron core during AC cycle dissipate energy as heat.

Minimization: Use soft iron core material with narrow hysteresis loop.

4. Flux Leakage Loss

Not all magnetic flux generated by primary winding links completely with secondary winding.

Minimization: Wind primary and secondary coils directly over each other on a shell-type core.

5. Humming Loss (Acoustic Loss)

The alternating magnetic field causes magnetostriction (periodic contraction and expansion of iron core), producing continuous mechanical vibration and audible humming noise.

Transformer Efficiency (\(\eta\)) Formula

\[\eta = \frac{\text{Output Power } (P_s)}{\text{Input Power } (P_p)} \times 100\% = \frac{V_s I_s \cos\phi_s}{V_p I_p \cos\phi_p} \times 100\%\]

For standard practical transformers, efficiency ranges between 90% and 98%.

NEB 2078 Board Exam Question [5 Marks]

Question 1: A coil of \(500\) turns and radius \(4\text{ cm}\) is rotated at \(1200\text{ rpm}\) in a uniform magnetic field of \(0.05\text{ T}\) perpendicular to the axis of rotation. Calculate: (i) the maximum EMF induced, (ii) the instantaneous EMF when the plane of coil makes an angle of \(30^\circ\) with the magnetic field.

Solution:

Given Data:

Turns \(N = 500\), Radius \(r = 0.04\text{ m} \implies \text{Area } A = \pi r^2 = \pi (0.04)^2 = 5.026 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2\)

Frequency of rotation \(f = \frac{1200}{60} = 20\text{ rev/s} \implies \omega = 2\pi f = 2 \pi (20) = 40\pi \approx 125.66\text{ rad/s}\)

Magnetic field \(B = 0.05\text{ T}\)

(i) Maximum (Peak) EMF (\(e_0\)):

\[e_0 = N B A \omega = 500 \times 0.05 \times (5.026 \times 10^{-3}) \times 125.66 = 15.79\text{ Volts}\]

(ii) Instantaneous EMF when plane makes \(30^\circ\) with \(B\):

Note: Angle \(\theta\) in \(e = e_0 \sin\theta\) is between magnetic field and normal vector to coil plane. Thus, \(\theta = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ\).

\[e = e_0 \sin(60^\circ) = 15.79 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 15.79 \times 0.866 = 13.67\text{ Volts}\]

NEB 2076 Board Exam Question [4 Marks]

Question 2: A step-down transformer connected to \(220\text{ V}\) AC line supplies \(11\text{ V}\) to a load of resistance \(2\ \Omega\). If the efficiency of the transformer is \(90\%\), calculate: (i) secondary current, (ii) output power, (iii) primary current.

Solution:

Given: \(V_p = 220\text{ V}\), \(V_s = 11\text{ V}\), \(R_L = 2\ \Omega\), Efficiency \(\eta = 90\% = 0.90\)

(i) Secondary Current (\(I_s\)):

\[I_s = \frac{V_s}{R_L} = \frac{11}{2} = 5.5\text{ A}\]

(ii) Output Power (\(P_s\)):

\[P_s = V_s \times I_s = 11 \times 5.5 = 60.5\text{ Watts}\]

(iii) Primary Current (\(I_p\)):

\[\eta = \frac{P_s}{P_p} \implies P_p = \frac{P_s}{\eta} = \frac{60.5}{0.90} = 67.22\text{ Watts}\] \[P_p = V_p \times I_p \implies I_p = \frac{P_p}{V_p} = \frac{67.22}{220} = 0.305\text{ A}\]

NEB 2074 Board Exam Question [3 Marks]

Question 3: An air-cored solenoid of length \(50\text{ cm}\) and radius \(2\text{ cm}\) has \(1000\) turns. Calculate its self-inductance and the energy stored in it when a current of \(5\text{ A}\) flows through it. (\(\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\text{ H/m}\)).

Solution:

Given: \(l = 0.5\text{ m}\), \(r = 0.02\text{ m} \implies A = \pi (0.02)^2 = 1.257 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2\), \(N = 1000\), \(I = 5\text{ A}\)

Self-Inductance (\(L\)):

\[L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times (1000)^2 \times (1.257 \times 10^{-3})}{0.5} = 3.16 \times 10^{-3}\text{ H} = 3.16\text{ mH}\]

Stored Energy (\(U\)):

\[U = \frac{1}{2} L I^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times (3.16 \times 10^{-3}) \times (5)^2 = 0.0395\text{ Joules}\]

High-Frequency Conceptual Short Answer Questions

A choke coil is a high-inductance coil with very low ohmic resistance. The average power consumed in an ideal inductor over a complete AC cycle is zero (\(\Phi = 90^\circ \implies P = V I \cos 90^\circ = 0\)). Thus, a choke coil reduces current without any significant loss of electrical energy as heat. In contrast, a resistor reduces current by dissipating electrical energy into heat (\(I^2 R\)), leading to continuous energy waste.

Due to high self-inductance (\(L\)), a massive self-induced back EMF (\(e = -L \frac{dI}{dt}\)) is produced across the tiny air gap between switch contacts. This huge electric field ionizes the air molecules in the gap, causing electric breakdown visible as a spark.

Connecting a steady DC voltage across primary turns may even burn out the winding due to high current.
