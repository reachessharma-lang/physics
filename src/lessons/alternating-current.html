19.1 Peak and RMS Value of AC Current and Voltage

An alternating current is a current whose magnitude and direction change periodically with time. A sinusoidally varying alternating current may be written as

\[ i=I_0\sin\omega t \]where \(I_0\) is the peak current and \(\omega=2\pi f\) is the angular frequency.

Similarly, an alternating voltage is

\[ v=V_0\sin\omega t \]where \(V_0\) is the peak voltage.

Peak value

Peak value is the maximum instantaneous value attained by an alternating current or voltage during a cycle.

The peak-to-peak value is

\[ I_{\mathrm{pp}}=2I_0,\qquad V_{\mathrm{pp}}=2V_0 \]\(I_0\) and \(V_0\) are peak current and peak voltage.

RMS value

The rms value of an alternating current is the value of steady direct current that produces the same heating effect in a given resistance as the alternating current over one complete cycle.

For \(i=I_0\sin\omega t\), the mean square current is

\[ I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2=\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T I_0^2\sin^2\omega t\,dt =\frac{I_0^2}{2} \]

Therefore,

\[ I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{I_0}{\sqrt2} \]For a sinusoidal alternating current.

Similarly,

\[ V_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{V_0}{\sqrt2} \]

Important: The domestic AC voltage quoted as 220 V or 230 V is normally its rms value, not its peak value.

Relation between peak and rms values

\[ I_0=\sqrt2\,I_{\mathrm{rms}},\qquad V_0=\sqrt2\,V_{\mathrm{rms}} \]

Worked Example 1

Problem: An AC current has a peak value of 10 A. Calculate its rms value.

Show solution

Using

\[ I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{I_0}{\sqrt2} \]
\[ I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{10}{\sqrt2}=7.07\ \mathrm{A} \]

Answer: \(I_{\mathrm{rms}}=7.07\,A\).

19.2 AC Through a Resistor, Inductor and Capacitor

AC through a pure resistor

For a resistor, Ohm's law is

\[ v=iR \]

If \(i=I_0\sin\omega t\), then

\[ v=RI_0\sin\omega t=V_0\sin\omega t \]

Thus voltage and current are in phase.

\[ Z_R=R,\qquad \phi=0 \]

In a pure resistor: voltage and current are in phase and the impedance is equal to the resistance.

AC through a pure inductor

For an ideal inductor,

\[ v=L\frac{di}{dt} \]

Let

\[ i=I_0\sin\omega t \]

Then

\[ v=\omega LI_0\cos\omega t =V_0\sin\left(\omega t+\frac{\pi}{2}\right) \]

Therefore, voltage leads current by \(90^\circ\), or current lags voltage by \(90^\circ\).

Inductive reactance

\[ X_L=\omega L=2\pi fL \]\(X_L\) is inductive reactance in ohms.

The impedance of an ideal inductor is

\[ Z_L=X_L=\omega L \]

As frequency increases, \(X_L\) increases. Therefore, an inductor offers greater opposition to high-frequency AC.

AC through a pure capacitor

For a capacitor,

\[ i=C\frac{dv}{dt} \]

If

\[ v=V_0\sin\omega t \]

then

\[ i=\omega CV_0\cos\omega t =I_0\sin\left(\omega t+\frac{\pi}{2}\right) \]

Thus current leads voltage by \(90^\circ\).

Capacitive reactance

\[ X_C=\frac{1}{\omega C} =\frac{1}{2\pi fC} \]\(X_C\) is capacitive reactance in ohms.

The impedance of an ideal capacitor is

\[ Z_C=X_C \]

As frequency increases, \(X_C\) decreases. Therefore, a capacitor offers less opposition to high-frequency AC.

Phase relationships

Circuit elementPhase relationReactance / impedance
ResistorV and I in phase\(Z=R\)
InductorV leads I by \(90^\circ\)\(X_L=\omega L\)
CapacitorI leads V by \(90^\circ\)\(X_C=1/(\omega C)\)

19.3 Phasor Diagrams and Impedance of L-R, R-C and L-C Series Circuits

A phasor is a rotating vector used to represent the magnitude and phase of an alternating quantity.

L-R series circuit

Since the same current flows through \(R\) and \(L\), take current \(I\) as the reference phasor.

The resistor voltage \(V_R\) is in phase with \(I\), while \(V_L\) leads \(I\) by \(90^\circ\).

From the right triangle,

\[ V=\sqrt{V_R^2+V_L^2} \]

Since \(V_R=IR\) and \(V_L=IX_L\),

\[ V=I\sqrt{R^2+X_L^2} \]

Therefore, impedance is

\[ \boxed{Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_L^2}} =\sqrt{R^2+(\omega L)^2} \]Impedance \(Z\) is measured in ohms.

The phase angle is

\[ \tan\phi=\frac{V_L}{V_R} =\frac{X_L}{R} =\frac{\omega L}{R} \]

Hence

\[ I=\frac{V}{Z} \]

R-C series circuit

Take current \(I\) as the reference phasor. \(V_R\) is in phase with \(I\), whereas \(V_C\) lags \(I\) by \(90^\circ\).

The magnitude of the supply voltage is

\[ V=\sqrt{V_R^2+V_C^2} \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_C^2}} =\sqrt{R^2+\frac{1}{(\omega C)^2}} \]

The phase angle is

\[ \tan\phi=-\frac{X_C}{R} \]

The negative sign indicates that current leads voltage.

L-C series circuit

In an L-C series circuit, \(V_L\) and \(V_C\) are \(180^\circ\) out of phase.

\[ V=|V_L-V_C| =I|X_L-X_C| \]

Thus, for an ideal L-C circuit,

\[ \boxed{Z=|X_L-X_C|} \]

At \(X_L=X_C\),

\[ Z=0 \]

for the ideal circuit. In a real circuit, unavoidable resistance prevents the impedance from becoming zero.

Comparison of series circuits

CircuitImpedanceNature

L-C\(Z=|X_L-X_C|\)Inductive or capacitive

Worked Example 2 — R-L circuit

Problem: A resistor of \(30\,\Omega\) and an inductor of \(0.20\,H\) are connected in series to a \(50\,Hz\) AC supply. Find the impedance and current when the rms voltage is \(120\,V\).

Show solution

Given \(R=30\,\Omega,\ L=0.20\,H,\ f=50\,Hz,\ V=120\,V\).

\[ X_L=2\pi fL=2\pi(50)(0.20)=62.83\,\Omega \]
\[ Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_L^2} =\sqrt{30^2+62.83^2} =69.62\,\Omega \]
\[ I=\frac{V}{Z} =\frac{120}{69.62} =1.72\,A \]

Answer: \(Z\approx69.6\,\Omega\), \(I\approx1.72\,A\).

Worked Example 3 — R-C circuit

Problem: A \(1000\,\Omega\) resistor and a \(2\,\mu F\) capacitor are connected in series to a \(12\,V\), \(50\,Hz\) AC supply. Find the capacitive reactance, impedance and current.

Show solution
\[ X_C=\frac{1}{2\pi fC} =\frac{1}{2\pi(50)(2\times10^{-6})} =1591.5\,\Omega \]
\[ Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_C^2} =\sqrt{1000^2+1591.5^2} \approx1879.4\,\Omega \]
\[ I=\frac{12}{1879.4} \approx6.39\times10^{-3}\,A \]

Answer: \(X_C\approx1592\,\Omega,\ Z\approx1879\,\Omega,\ I\approx6.39\,mA\).

19.4 Series LCR Circuit and Impedance

In a series LCR circuit, the same current \(I\) passes through \(R\), \(L\) and \(C\).

The voltage across the resistor is

\[ V_R=IR \]

The voltage across the inductor is

\[ V_L=IX_L \]

The voltage across the capacitor is

\[ V_C=IX_C \]

Here \(V_L\) and \(V_C\) act in opposite directions in the phasor diagram.

The net reactive voltage is

\[ V_X=V_L-V_C=I(X_L-X_C) \]

Hence the supply voltage is

\[ V=\sqrt{V_R^2+(V_L-V_C)^2} \]

Substituting the voltage expressions,

\[ V=I\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2} \]

Therefore, the impedance of a series LCR circuit is

\[ \boxed{Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}} \]\(Z\) is the impedance, \(R\) is resistance, \(X_L=\omega L\), and \(X_C=1/(\omega C)\).

Thus,

\[ \boxed{Z=\sqrt{R^2+\left(\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}} \]

Current in an LCR circuit

\[ \boxed{I=\frac{V}{Z}} \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ I= \frac{V} {\sqrt{R^2+\left(\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}} } \]

Phase angle in an LCR circuit

\[ \boxed{\tan\phi=\frac{X_L-X_C}{R}} \]
  • If \(X_L>X_C\), the circuit is inductive and current lags voltage.
  • If \(X_C>X_L\), the circuit is capacitive and current leads voltage.
  • If \(X_L=X_C\), the circuit is purely resistive at resonance.

Impedance triangle

\[ Z^2=R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2 \]

Worked Example 4 — NEB-style LCR impedance problem

Problem: A series LCR circuit consists of \(R=300\,\Omega\), \(L=60\,mH\) and \(C=0.50\,\mu F\), connected to a \(50\,Hz\) AC supply. Calculate the impedance.

Show solution

Given:

\(R=300\,\Omega,\ L=60\times10^{-3}\,H,\ C=0.50\times10^{-6}\,F,\ f=50\,Hz\).

\[ \omega=2\pi f=314.16\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}} \]
\[ X_L=\omega L =(314.16)(0.060) =18.85\,\Omega \]
\[ X_C=\frac{1}{\omega C} =\frac{1}{(314.16)(0.50\times10^{-6})} =6366.2\,\Omega \]
\[ Z=\sqrt{300^2+(18.85-6366.2)^2} \]
\[ Z\approx6354\,\Omega \]

Answer: \(Z\approx6.35\,k\Omega\). The circuit is strongly capacitive because \(X_C>X_L\).

19.5 Series Resonance

Resonance in a series LCR circuit is the condition in which the inductive reactance becomes equal to the capacitive reactance.

The resonance condition is

\[ X_L=X_C \]

Therefore,

\[ \omega L=\frac{1}{\omega C} \]
\[ \omega^2=\frac{1}{LC} \]

Hence the angular resonant frequency is

\[ \boxed{\omega_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}} \]

Since \(\omega_0=2\pi f_0\),

\[ \boxed{f_0=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}} \]\(f_0\) is the resonant frequency.

Characteristics at resonance

  • \(X_L=X_C\).
  • Net reactance is zero.
  • \(Z=R\).
  • Current is maximum: \(I_{\max}=V/R\).
  • Voltage and current are in phase.
  • Power factor is unity.
  • Average power is maximum.
  • The voltages across \(L\) and \(C\) are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase.

Worked Example 5 — Resonant frequency

Problem: A series LCR circuit has \(L=0.20\,H\) and \(C=50\,\mu F\). Find its resonant frequency.

Show solution
\[ f_0=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} \]
\[ f_0= \frac{1} {2\pi\sqrt{(0.20)(50\times10^{-6})}} \]
\[ f_0\approx50.3\,Hz \]

Answer: \(f_0\approx50.3\,Hz\).

Applications of resonance

  • Tuning radio and television receivers.
  • Selection of a desired frequency from many frequencies.
  • Oscillator and communication circuits.
  • Frequency-selective electronic circuits.

19.6 Quality Factor

The quality factor or \(Q\)-factor of a series resonant circuit indicates the sharpness of resonance.

For a series LCR circuit,

\[ \boxed{Q=\frac{\omega_0L}{R}} \]

Since \(\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}\),

\[ \boxed{ Q=\frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} } \]

A high \(Q\)-factor means a sharper resonance curve and better frequency selectivity.

Bandwidth

\[ \boxed{Q=\frac{f_0}{\Delta f}} \]\(\Delta f=f_2-f_1\) is the bandwidth between the half-power frequencies.

Higher \(Q\) means narrower bandwidth and sharper resonance.

Worked Example 6 — Quality factor

Problem: A series LCR circuit has \(L=0.20\,H\), \(C=50\,\mu F\) and \(R=2\,\Omega\). Calculate its quality factor.

Show solution
\[ Q=\frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} \]
\[ Q=\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{0.20}{50\times10^{-6}}} \]
\[ Q\approx31.6 \]

Answer: \(Q\approx31.6\).

19.7 Power in AC Circuits and Power Factor

In an AC circuit, voltage and current may not be in phase. Let

\[ v=V_0\sin\omega t \]

and

\[ i=I_0\sin(\omega t-\phi) \]

The instantaneous power is

\[ p=vi \]

The average power consumed by the circuit is

\[ \boxed{P=V_{\mathrm{rms}}I_{\mathrm{rms}}\cos\phi} \]

The quantity \(\cos\phi\) is called the power factor.

Power factor is the cosine of the phase angle between the voltage and current in an AC circuit.

\[ \boxed{\mathrm{power\ factor}=\cos\phi} \]

For a series LCR circuit,

\[ \boxed{\cos\phi=\frac{R}{Z}} \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{P=I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2R} \]

for a series LCR circuit because only the resistance consumes average power.

Special cases

CircuitPower factorAverage power
Pure resistor1\(VI\)
Pure inductor00
Pure capacitor00
LCR at resonance1\(VI\)

Worked Example 7 — AC power

Problem: An AC circuit operates at \(230\,V\) rms and carries a current of \(5\,A\). If its power factor is \(0.8\), calculate the average power consumed.

Show solution
\[ P=V_{\mathrm{rms}}I_{\mathrm{rms}}\cos\phi \]
\[ P=(230)(5)(0.8)=920\,W \]

Answer: \(P=920\,W\).

Common Mistakes in NEB Examinations

  • Do not write \(X_L=1/\omega L\). The correct expression is \(X_L=\omega L\).
  • Do not write \(X_C=\omega C\). The correct expression is \(X_C=1/\omega C\).
  • Do not add \(X_L\) and \(X_C\) directly in an LCR impedance calculation. Their difference is used.
  • Do not write \(Z=R+X_L-X_C\). The correct magnitude is \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\).
  • At resonance, \(X_L=X_C\), not \(X_L=X_C=0\).
  • At resonance, \(Z=R\), not \(Z=0\) for a practical LCR circuit.
  • In a pure inductor, current lags voltage by \(90^\circ\).
  • In a pure capacitor, current leads voltage by \(90^\circ\).
  • Use rms voltage and rms current together in \(P=VI\cos\phi\).
  • Keep \(mH\) and \(\mu F\) conversions correct before calculating impedance or resonance frequency.

Key Points for Revision

  • AC quantities are generally represented by sinusoidal equations.
  • The rms value of a sinusoidal AC is \(1/\sqrt2\) times its peak value.
  • In a resistor, voltage and current are in phase.
  • In an inductor, voltage leads current by \(90^\circ\).
  • In a capacitor, current leads voltage by \(90^\circ\).
  • Inductive reactance increases with frequency.
  • Capacitive reactance decreases with frequency.
  • For an L-R circuit, \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_L^2}\).
  • For an R-C circuit, \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_C^2}\).
  • For an ideal L-C circuit, \(Z=|X_L-X_C|\).
  • For an LCR series circuit, \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\).
  • Resonance occurs when \(X_L=X_C\).
  • At resonance, \(Z=R\), current is maximum and power factor is unity.
  • The resonant frequency is \(f_0=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})\).
  • A high quality factor means sharper resonance and smaller bandwidth.
  • Average AC power is \(P=VI\cos\phi\).
