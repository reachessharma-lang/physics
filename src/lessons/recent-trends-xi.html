26.1 Elementary Particles and Antiparticles

Elementary particles: Elementary particles are particles that are not known to be made up of smaller constituent particles. They are the fundamental building blocks involved in matter and interactions.

Examples of elementary particles include quarks, leptons, and the particles that mediate fundamental interactions.

Antiparticles

Antiparticle: An antiparticle is the corresponding antimatter partner of a particle, having the same mass and spin as the particle but opposite values of its additive quantum numbers, such as electric charge where applicable.

Examples include:

  • Electron \(e^-\) and positron \(e^+\).
  • Proton \(p\) and antiproton \(\bar p\).
  • Neutron \(n\) and antineutron \(\bar n\).

The positron is the antiparticle of the electron. It has the same mass as the electron but carries positive electric charge.

\[ m_{e^-}=m_{e^+} \]
\[ q_{e^-}=-e,\qquad q_{e^+}=+e \]

Particle-Antiparticle Annihilation

When a particle meets its antiparticle, they can annihilate and their mass-energy can be converted into other particles.

\[ e^-+e^+\rightarrow\gamma+\gamma \]

For an electron and positron initially at rest, the total rest energy is approximately \(1.022\,\mathrm{MeV}\), which can appear as the energy of the two photons.

Matter and antimatter are not simply positive and negative versions of every particle. For example, the neutron has zero electric charge, but it still has a distinct antiparticle, the antineutron.

26.2 Classification of Particles

Particles can be classified according to their fundamental nature and the interactions in which they participate.

Major Classification

Category Main characteristics Examples
Quarks Elementary particles that combine to form hadrons up, down, charm, strange, top, bottom
Leptons Elementary particles that do not experience the strong nuclear interaction electron, muon, tau, neutrinos
Hadrons Composite particles made of quarks protons, neutrons, mesons
Gauge bosons Particles associated with fundamental interactions photon, gluons, \(W^\pm\), \(Z^0\)

Hadrons

Hadrons: Particles made of quarks and bound by the strong interaction are called hadrons.

Hadrons are mainly divided into two groups:

  • Baryons: Made of three quarks.
  • Mesons: Made of one quark and one antiquark.
Important distinction: Quarks and leptons are elementary particles in the Standard Model, whereas protons and neutrons are composite particles made of quarks.

26.3 Quarks: Names, Symbols and Charges

Quarks: Quarks are elementary particles that combine to form hadrons such as protons, neutrons, mesons and many other strongly interacting particles.

There are six flavours of quarks:

Quark Symbol Electric charge
Up \(u\)
Down \(d\) \(-\frac{1}{3}e\)
Charm \(c\) \(+\frac{2}{3}e\)
Strange \(s\) \(-\frac{1}{3}e\)
Top \(t\) \(+\frac{2}{3}e\)
Bottom \(b\) \(-\frac{1}{3}e\)

Each quark has a corresponding antiquark. The antiquark has the opposite electric charge and opposite relevant additive quantum numbers.

Quark Antiquark
\(u\) \(\bar u\)
\(d\) \(\bar d\)
\(c\) \(\bar c\)
\(s\) \(\bar s\)
\(t\) \(\bar t\)
\(b\) \(\bar b\)
A useful memory rule is: \(u,c,t\) have charge \(+\frac{2}{3}e\), while \(d,s,b\) have charge \(-\frac{1}{3}e\).

26.4 Quark Combinations of Mesons and Baryons

Mesons

Mesons: Mesons are hadrons consisting of a quark and an antiquark.
\[ \boxed{\text{Meson}=q\bar q} \]

Examples of Mesons

Meson Quark composition Charge
\(\pi^+\) \(u\bar d\) \(+e\)
\(\pi^-\) \(d\bar u\) \(-e\)
\(\pi^0\) \(\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(u\bar u-d\bar d)\) \(0\)
\(K^+\) \(u\bar s\) \(+e\)
\(K^-\) \(s\bar u\) \(-e\)

Baryons

Baryons: Baryons are hadrons consisting of three quarks.
\[ \boxed{\text{Baryon}=qqq} \]

Examples of Baryons

Particle Quark composition Charge
Proton \(p\) \(uud\) \(+e\)
Neutron \(n\) \(udd\) \(0\)
\(\Delta^{++}\) \(uuu\) \(+2e\)

For example, the charge of a proton is obtained from its quark composition:

\[ q_p=\frac{2}{3}e+\frac{2}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e=e \]

Similarly, for a neutron:

\[ q_n=\frac{2}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e=0 \]
Antibaryons consist of three antiquarks, while antimesons consist of an antiquark and a quark.

26.5 Leptons

Leptons: Leptons are elementary particles that do not participate in the strong nuclear interaction.

The six types of leptons are:

Lepton Symbol Charge
Electron \(e^-\) \(-e\)
Electron neutrino \(\nu_e\) 0
Muon \(\mu^-\) \(-e\)
Muon neutrino \(\nu_\mu\) 0
Tau \(\tau^-\) \(-e\)
Tau neutrino \(\nu_\tau\) 0

Every charged lepton has a corresponding antilepton. Neutrinos also have corresponding antineutrinos.

Important Properties of Leptons

  • Leptons are elementary particles.
  • They do not experience the strong nuclear interaction.
  • Charged leptons participate in electromagnetic and weak interactions.
  • Neutrinos interact primarily through the weak interaction and gravity.
  • The electron is the lightest charged lepton.
For examination purposes, remember the three charged leptons as electron, muon and tau, and the corresponding three neutrinos as electron neutrino, muon neutrino and tau neutrino.

26.6 Big Bang, Hubble's Law and Expansion of the Universe

Big Bang Theory

Big Bang theory: The Big Bang model states that the universe began from an extremely hot and dense early state and has been expanding and cooling with time.

The Big Bang should not be visualized simply as an explosion of matter into pre-existing empty space. Rather, the large-scale distances between galaxies increase as space itself expands.

Main Evidence for the Big Bang

  • Expansion of the universe.
  • Cosmic microwave background radiation.
  • Observed abundance of light elements such as hydrogen and helium.

Hubble's Law

Edwin Hubble observed that distant galaxies generally show a redshift and that more distant galaxies recede faster from us.

Hubble's law: The recession velocity of a galaxy is approximately proportional to its distance from the observer.
\[ \boxed{v=H_0d} \] where \(v\) is recession velocity, \(d\) is distance from the observer and \(H_0\) is the Hubble constant.

For sufficiently small cosmological redshifts, the recession velocity can be related to redshift by

\[ v\approx cz \] where \(c\) is the speed of light and \(z\) is the redshift.

Combining these relations gives

\[ cz\approx H_0d \]

Why Does Hubble's Law Indicate Expansion?

If galaxies are moving away from one another and the recession velocity increases with distance, the large-scale universe is expanding.

The expansion is a large-scale property of the universe. Local systems such as atoms, solar systems and gravitationally bound galaxies do not simply expand along with the universe in the same way.

26.7 Dark Matter, Black Holes and Gravitational Waves

Dark Matter

Dark matter: Dark matter is matter that does not emit, absorb or reflect sufficient electromagnetic radiation to be directly detected by ordinary telescopes, but whose gravitational effects can be observed.

Evidence for dark matter comes from several gravitational observations, including:

  • Rotation speeds of galaxies.
  • Motion of galaxies in clusters.
  • Gravitational lensing.
  • Large-scale structure of the universe.

Dark matter is called “dark” because it does not interact significantly with electromagnetic radiation in a way that allows it to be seen directly.

Black Hole

Black hole: A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that, within the event horizon, nothing can escape to distant observers, including light.

Event Horizon

Event horizon: The boundary surrounding a black hole beyond which escape to the outside universe is impossible is called the event horizon.

A black hole can form when a sufficiently massive star reaches the end of its evolution and its core collapses under gravity, although black holes can also form through other astrophysical processes.

A black hole itself does not shine like an ordinary star. However, matter falling toward a black hole can become extremely hot and emit intense radiation before crossing the event horizon.

Gravitational Waves

Gravitational waves: Gravitational waves are ripples in the curvature of spacetime that propagate outward from accelerating masses with changing quadrupole moments.

They were predicted by Einstein's general theory of relativity. Strong gravitational-wave signals can be produced by violent astrophysical events such as:

  • Merger of black holes.
  • Merger of neutron stars.
  • Other asymmetric compact-object systems.

Gravitational waves travel through space at the speed of light in vacuum.

Detection of Gravitational Waves

Gravitational waves can be detected by measuring extremely small changes in the relative lengths of perpendicular arms of laser interferometers.

The first direct detection of gravitational waves was announced in 2016 from a signal produced by the merger of two black holes. This provided strong experimental confirmation of a major prediction of general relativity.

Conceptual Questions and Clear Interpretations

Why are quarks called elementary particles?

Quarks are called elementary because, according to the Standard Model, they are not composed of smaller known constituents.

Why are protons and neutrons not elementary particles?

Protons and neutrons are hadrons. They are composite particles made primarily of three valence quarks held together by the strong interaction.

Why is the proton positively charged?

A proton has quark composition \(uud\). Therefore, \[ q_p=\frac{2}{3}e+\frac{2}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e=+e. \]

Why is the neutron electrically neutral?

A neutron has quark composition \(udd\). Therefore, \[ q_n=\frac{2}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e=0. \]

Why does Hubble's law support the expansion of the universe?

Hubble's law states \(v=H_0d\). Thus, the greater the distance of a galaxy, the greater its observed recession velocity. This systematic recession of distant galaxies is evidence that the universe is expanding.

Why is dark matter called “dark”?

Dark matter does not produce or interact sufficiently with electromagnetic radiation for direct observation by ordinary telescopes. Its existence is inferred mainly from its gravitational effects.

Can light escape from inside a black hole?

No. Inside the event horizon, spacetime is curved so strongly that all possible future-directed paths lead inward; light cannot escape to distant observers.

What is the difference between a gravitational wave and an ordinary electromagnetic wave?

An electromagnetic wave is an oscillation of electric and magnetic fields, whereas a gravitational wave is a propagating disturbance in spacetime curvature. Gravitational waves are generated by changing mass distributions with accelerating quadrupole moments.
