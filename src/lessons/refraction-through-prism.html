15. Refraction Through Prisms

A prism is a transparent refracting medium bounded by two plane refracting surfaces inclined to each other at an angle. The angle between the two refracting surfaces is called the angle of prism.

When a ray of light passes through a prism, it undergoes refraction at both refracting surfaces. As a result, the emergent ray is deviated from its original direction.

Angle of prism \(A\): The angle between the two refracting faces of a prism.

Angle of deviation \(\delta\): The angle through which the emergent ray is deviated from the original direction of the incident ray.

Basic Geometry of a Prism

Important Relations for a Prism

From the geometry of the prism, the angle of prism is related to the angles of refraction inside the prism by

\[ \boxed{A=r_1+r_2} \]

The total deviation produced by the prism is

\[ \boxed{\delta=i+e-A} \]

Here, \(i\) is the angle of incidence, \(e\) is the angle of emergence, \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) are the angles of refraction inside the prism, \(A\) is the angle of prism, and \(\delta\) is the angle of deviation.

15.1 Minimum Deviation Condition

As the angle of incidence \(i\) is gradually increased, the angle of deviation \(\delta\) initially decreases, reaches a minimum value, and then increases again.

Angle of minimum deviation \(\delta_m\): The minimum value of the angle of deviation produced by a prism for a particular wavelength of light is called the angle of minimum deviation.

At the condition of minimum deviation, the path of the ray through the prism becomes symmetrical.

Condition for minimum deviation:

\[ \boxed{i=e} \]

Consequently, the two angles of refraction inside the prism are equal:

\[ \boxed{r_1=r_2} \]

Since

\[ A=r_1+r_2 \]

At minimum deviation, \(r_1=r_2=r\), so

\[ A=2r \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{r=\frac{A}{2}} \]

Also, using

\[ \delta=i+e-A \]

and \(i=e=i_m\) at minimum deviation,

\[ \delta_m=2i_m-A \]

Hence,

\[ \boxed{i_m=\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}} \]

Symmetrical Path at Minimum Deviation

Why Is the Path Symmetrical at Minimum Deviation?

The angle of deviation depends on the angle of incidence. When the prism is rotated through the position of minimum deviation, the incident and emergent rays make equal angles with the corresponding normals.

\[ i=e \]

Since

\[ A=r_1+r_2 \]

and \(r_1=r_2\), we obtain

\[ r_1=r_2=\frac{A}{2} \]

Thus, the path of the light ray inside the prism is symmetrical about the plane bisecting the prism angle.

Deviation Curve

The variation of the angle of deviation \(\delta\) with the angle of incidence \(i\) is represented by a deviation curve.

Exam point: At minimum deviation, the deviation is minimum and the ray passes symmetrically through the prism. Therefore, \(i=e\) and \(r_1=r_2=A/2\).

15.2 Relation Between Angle of Prism, Angle of Minimum Deviation and Refractive Index

Let \(A\) be the angle of the prism, \(\delta_m\) the angle of minimum deviation, \(i_m\) the angle of incidence at minimum deviation, and \(r\) the angle of refraction inside the prism.

At minimum deviation:

\[ i=e=i_m \]

and

\[ r_1=r_2=r \]

Therefore,

\[ A=r_1+r_2=2r \]

Hence,

\[ \boxed{r=\frac{A}{2}} \]

Also,

\[ \delta_m=i_m+e-A \]

Since \(i_m=e\),

\[ \delta_m=2i_m-A \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{i_m=\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}} \]

Derivation of Refractive Index of the Prism

Applying Snell's law at the first refracting surface:

\[ \mu=\frac{\sin i_m}{\sin r} \]

Using

\[ i_m=\frac{A+\delta_m}{2} \]

and

\[ r=\frac{A}{2} \]

we obtain

\[ \boxed{ \mu= \frac{ \sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right) }{ \sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right) } } \]

Prism formula at minimum deviation:

\[ \boxed{ \mu= \frac{ \sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right) }{ \sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right) } } \]

This equation is used to determine the refractive index of the material of a prism when its angle \(A\) and angle of minimum deviation \(\delta_m\) are known.

Special Case: Equilateral Prism

For an equilateral prism,

\[ A=60^\circ \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ \mu= \frac{\sin\left(\frac{60^\circ+\delta_m}{2}\right)} {\sin30^\circ} } \]

Since \(\sin30^\circ=1/2\),

\[ \boxed{ \mu=2\sin\left(\frac{60^\circ+\delta_m}{2}\right) } \]

Worked Example 1: Finding Refractive Index

Question: A prism has an angle of \(60^\circ\) and produces a minimum deviation of \(40^\circ\). Find the refractive index of its material.

Show solution ```

Using the prism formula,

\[ \mu= \frac{ \sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right) }{ \sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right) } \]

Substituting \(A=60^\circ\) and \(\delta_m=40^\circ\):

\[ \mu= \frac{\sin50^\circ}{\sin30^\circ} \]
\[ \mu= \frac{0.7660}{0.5} \approx1.532 \]

Answer: The refractive index of the prism material is approximately \(1.53\).

```

Worked Example 2: Finding Minimum Deviation

Question: A prism has an angle of \(60^\circ\) and refractive index \(1.5\). Find its angle of minimum deviation.

Show solution ```

Using

\[ \mu= \frac{ \sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right) }{ \sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right) } \]

For \(A=60^\circ\):

\[ 1.5= \frac{ \sin\left(\frac{60^\circ+\delta_m}{2}\right) }{\sin30^\circ} \]

Since \(\sin30^\circ=0.5\),

\[ \sin\left(\frac{60^\circ+\delta_m}{2}\right)=0.75 \]

Therefore,

\[ \frac{60^\circ+\delta_m}{2} =\sin^{-1}(0.75) \approx48.59^\circ \]
\[ \delta_m=2(48.59^\circ)-60^\circ \]
\[ \boxed{\delta_m\approx37.18^\circ} \]

Answer: The angle of minimum deviation is approximately \(37.2^\circ\).

```

Worked Example 3: Finding the Angle of Refraction at Minimum Deviation

Question: A prism has an angle of \(50^\circ\). Find the angle of refraction inside the prism when the prism is in the condition of minimum deviation.

Show solution ```

At minimum deviation,

\[ r_1=r_2 \]

Also,

\[ A=r_1+r_2=2r \]

Therefore,

\[ r=\frac{A}{2} =\frac{50^\circ}{2} =25^\circ \]

Answer: The angle of refraction at each face is \(25^\circ\).

```

15.3 Determination of Refractive Index of Prism Material

The refractive index of the material of a prism can be determined experimentally by measuring:

  • the angle of the prism \(A\), and
  • the angle of minimum deviation \(\delta_m\).

The refractive index is then calculated using

\[ \boxed{ \mu= \frac{ \sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right) }{ \sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right) } } \]

Experimental Idea

  1. Place the prism on a prism table.
  2. Allow a narrow beam of monochromatic light to fall on one face of the prism.
  3. Observe the emergent ray through a telescope.
  4. Rotate the prism gradually and observe the position of the emergent ray.
  5. The deviation decreases to a minimum value and then starts increasing.
  6. Record the angle corresponding to the minimum deviation.
  7. Measure the angle of the prism.
  8. Use the prism formula to calculate the refractive index.

Practical significance: The refractive index is a characteristic optical property of the prism material and depends on the wavelength of light and temperature.

Worked Example 4: Experimental Determination

Question: In an experiment, the angle of a prism is \(60^\circ\) and its measured angle of minimum deviation is \(42^\circ\). Determine the refractive index of the prism material.

Show solution ```
\[ \mu= \frac{ \sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right) }{ \sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right) } \]
\[ \mu= \frac{\sin\left(\frac{60^\circ+42^\circ}{2}\right)} {\sin30^\circ} \]
\[ \mu= \frac{\sin51^\circ}{0.5} \]
\[ \mu\approx\frac{0.7771}{0.5} \approx1.554 \]

Answer: The refractive index is approximately \(1.55\).

15.4 Deviation in a Small-Angle Prism

A prism whose refracting angle \(A\) is very small is called a small-angle prism or thin prism.

Small-angle prism: A prism having a very small refracting angle, for which the angles involved are sufficiently small that the small-angle approximation can be used.

For a small-angle prism, the angles of incidence, refraction and emergence are small. Hence, we can use

\[ \sin\theta\approx\theta \]

where \(\theta\) is expressed in radians.

Derivation of Deviation Produced by a Small-Angle Prism

For a prism,

\[ \delta=i+e-A \]

For a small-angle prism, using the small-angle approximation and Snell's law at the two surfaces:

\[ \mu-1\approx\frac{i-r_1}{r_1} \]

More directly, for the two refracting surfaces, the total deviation is

\[ \delta\approx(\mu-1)(r_1+r_2) \]

But

\[ r_1+r_2=A \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\delta=(\mu-1)A} \]

Deviation by a small-angle prism:

\[ \boxed{\delta=(\mu-1)A} \]

This important relation shows that the deviation produced by a thin prism is independent of the angle of incidence, provided the small-angle approximation is valid.

Small-Angle Prism Diagram

Worked Example 5: Deviation by a Small-Angle Prism

Question: A small-angle prism has an angle of \(4^\circ\) and refractive index \(1.5\). Find the deviation produced by it.

Show solution ```

For a small-angle prism,

\[ \delta=(\mu-1)A \]

Substituting \(\mu=1.5\) and \(A=4^\circ\):

\[ \delta=(1.5-1)\times4^\circ \]
\[ \boxed{\delta=2^\circ} \]

Answer: The deviation produced is \(2^\circ\).

```

Worked Example 6: Finding the Refractive Index from Small-Prism Deviation

Question: A small-angle prism of angle \(5^\circ\) produces a deviation of \(2^\circ\). Find its refractive index.

Show solution ```

Using

\[ \delta=(\mu-1)A \]

Therefore,

\[ \mu=1+\frac{\delta}{A} \]
\[ \mu=1+\frac{2}{5} \]
\[ \boxed{\mu=1.4} \]

Answer: The refractive index of the prism is \(1.4\).

```

Importance and Applications of Small-Angle Prisms

Small-angle prisms are important because they produce a small, controlled angular deviation of light. They are widely used in optical instruments and practical devices.

  • They are used for accurately deviating light rays by a small angle.
  • They are useful in optical instruments for changing the direction of light.
  • They are used in spectrometers and other optical arrangements.
  • They can be combined to produce desired angular deviation.
  • Prisms are used in binoculars and periscopes for redirecting light.
  • Prismatic optical elements are used in cameras and other precision optical systems.
  • The deviation of a prism can be used to determine the refractive index of transparent materials.

Important Formulae

Quantity Formula
Prism angle \(A=r_1+r_2\)
Deviation \(\delta=i+e-A\)
Minimum deviation condition \(i=e\)
Refraction angle at minimum deviation \(r_1=r_2=A/2\)
Angle of incidence at minimum deviation \(i_m=(A+\delta_m)/2\)
Refractive index \(\displaystyle \mu=\frac{\sin[(A+\delta_m)/2]}{\sin(A/2)}\)
Small-angle prism \(\delta=(\mu-1)A\)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Do not measure the angle of incidence from the prism face; it is measured from the normal.
  • Do not confuse the angle of prism \(A\) with the angle of deviation \(\delta\).
  • At minimum deviation, remember \(i=e\), not \(i=r\).
  • At minimum deviation, \(r_1=r_2=A/2\).
  • Use angles in consistent units when applying small-angle approximations. If using \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\), \(\theta\) must be in radians.
  • The formula \(\delta=(\mu-1)A\) applies to a small-angle prism under the small-angle approximation.
  • In the prism formula, use the angle of minimum deviation, not an arbitrary deviation.
  • For an equilateral prism, \(A=60^\circ\).
