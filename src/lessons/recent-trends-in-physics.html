25. Recent Trends in Physics

Recent developments in physics have expanded our understanding of the Earth, the universe, and matter at extremely small scales. This chapter introduces important modern areas of physics, including seismology, gravitational waves, nanotechnology, and the Higgs boson.

Seismology is the branch of physics and Earth science concerned with the study of earthquakes, seismic waves, their propagation, and the structure of the Earth's interior.

25.1 Seismology

(a) Origin of Earthquakes

An earthquake is the sudden shaking or vibration of the Earth's surface caused by the rapid release of energy stored within the Earth.

Most earthquakes originate because of the movement of tectonic plates. The Earth's outer rigid layer is divided into large plates that move slowly over the underlying mantle. When two plates move relative to each other, stress builds up in rocks along faults.

When the accumulated stress exceeds the strength of the rocks, the rocks suddenly break or slip along a fault. The stored elastic energy is released in the form of seismic waves, producing an earthquake.

Focus and Epicentre

  • Focus (hypocentre): The point inside the Earth where an earthquake originates and from where seismic energy is released.
  • Epicentre: The point on the Earth's surface vertically above the focus.
Exam point: The earthquake originates at the focus, whereas the epicentre is the corresponding point on the Earth's surface.

Seismic Waves

The energy released during an earthquake travels through the Earth and along its surface in the form of seismic waves. They are mainly classified as:

  1. Internal or body waves: P-waves and S-waves.
  2. Surface waves: Rayleigh waves and Love waves.

25.1(b) Surface Waves: Rayleigh and Love Waves

Surface waves are seismic waves that travel mainly along or near the Earth's surface. They generally produce stronger ground motion and cause considerable damage during earthquakes.

Rayleigh Waves

Rayleigh waves are surface seismic waves in which particles of the medium move in an approximately elliptical motion in a vertical plane containing the direction of wave propagation.

The motion resembles the movement of particles on the surface of a water wave. The particles move both vertically and horizontally, producing a rolling motion.

  • They travel along the Earth's surface.
  • Particle motion is approximately elliptical.
  • They have both vertical and horizontal components of motion.
  • Their amplitude decreases with depth.
  • They are generally slower than P-waves.
  • They can produce strong rolling motion and significant damage.

Love Waves

Love waves are surface seismic waves in which particles of the ground vibrate horizontally and perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.

  • They travel along the Earth's surface.
  • Particles vibrate horizontally and perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
  • They do not have significant vertical particle motion.
  • Their amplitude decreases with depth.
  • They are generally faster than Rayleigh waves but slower than body P-waves.
  • They can cause severe horizontal shaking of structures.

25.1(c) Internal Waves: S and P-Waves

Internal waves, also called body waves, travel through the interior of the Earth. The two principal types are P-waves and S-waves.

P-Waves

P-waves (primary waves) are longitudinal seismic waves in which particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation.

  • P-waves are longitudinal waves.
  • Particles vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation.
  • They produce alternate compressions and rarefactions.
  • They are the fastest seismic waves.
  • They can travel through solids, liquids and gases.
  • They are the first seismic waves recorded by a seismograph after an earthquake.
Important: Because P-waves can propagate through liquids, they can travel through the Earth's liquid outer core.

S-Waves

S-waves (secondary waves) are transverse seismic waves in which particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

  • S-waves are transverse waves.
  • Particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
  • They are slower than P-waves.
  • They can travel through solids but cannot travel through liquids or gases.
  • They are therefore not transmitted through the Earth's liquid outer core.
  • They arrive after P-waves at a seismic station.

25.1(d) Wave Patterns of the Gorkha Earthquake 2015

The Gorkha Earthquake of 25 April 2015 was a major earthquake in Nepal. Its epicentre was located in the Gorkha district, and the earthquake generated different types of seismic waves that were recorded by seismic stations in Nepal and surrounding regions.

The recorded seismic signal can be understood from the arrival of different seismic waves. The P-wave arrives first because it travels fastest through the Earth's interior. It is followed by the S-wave, which travels more slowly. Surface waves generally arrive later and can produce strong and prolonged ground motion.

Note: A real seismogram of the 2015 Gorkha Earthquake contains complex wave patterns because of the earthquake source, local geology, propagation path, reflections, refractions and surface effects. The diagram above is a simplified representation for examination purposes.

Importance of the Wave Pattern

  • The first prominent arrival corresponds to the P-wave.
  • The later arrival corresponds to the S-wave.
  • Surface waves follow the body waves and can have large amplitudes.
  • The difference in arrival times of P- and S-waves can be used to estimate the distance of a seismic station from the earthquake source.
  • Seismograms from several stations can be used to locate the epicentre.
Exam point: The greater the separation between P-wave and S-wave arrivals at a station, the farther the earthquake source is from that station, provided the appropriate seismic velocity model is used.

25.2(a) Gravitational Waves

Meaning

Gravitational waves are ripples or disturbances in the fabric of spacetime produced by accelerating massive objects, especially systems involving very strong and rapidly changing gravitational fields.

They are a prediction of Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity. Gravitational waves travel through space at the speed of light in vacuum.

Definition: A gravitational wave is a propagating disturbance in spacetime that carries energy away from an accelerating system with a changing quadrupole mass distribution.

Sources of Gravitational Waves

Strong detectable gravitational waves can be produced by violent astrophysical events such as:

  • merging black holes,
  • merging neutron stars,
  • collisions involving compact stellar objects, and
  • certain asymmetric motions of massive astronomical systems.

Effect of a Gravitational Wave

When a gravitational wave passes through a region of space, it produces extremely small changes in the distances between freely falling objects. The stretching occurs in one direction while compression occurs in the perpendicular direction, alternating as the wave passes.

Detection of Gravitational Waves

Gravitational waves are detected using extremely sensitive instruments called laser interferometers. Facilities such as LIGO use laser beams to measure extraordinarily small changes in the relative lengths of perpendicular arms.

The first direct detection of gravitational waves was announced in 2016, from a signal produced by the merger of two black holes. The observation provided strong direct evidence for a prediction of general relativity and opened the field of gravitational-wave astronomy.

Exam point: Gravitational waves are not ordinary mechanical waves. They are disturbances of spacetime itself and can travel through vacuum.

25.2(b) Nanotechnology

Meaning of Nanotechnology

Nanotechnology is the science and technology of understanding, controlling and manipulating matter at dimensions approximately between 1 and 100 nanometres.

Nanometre: One nanometre is equal to \(10^{-9}\,\text{m}\).
\[ 1\,\text{nm}=10^{-9}\,\text{m} \]where nm denotes nanometre.

At the nanoscale, materials can show properties that differ significantly from those of the same material at larger dimensions. These changes are associated with effects such as the large surface-area-to-volume ratio and quantum effects.

Why Nanomaterials Have Special Properties

  • They have a very large surface-area-to-volume ratio.
  • Quantum effects become important at very small dimensions.
  • Electrical, optical, magnetic and mechanical properties can change with particle size.
  • The properties of nanomaterials can often be controlled by changing their size and structure.

Applications of Nanotechnology

Medicine

  • Targeted drug delivery.
  • Medical imaging.
  • Development of sensors and diagnostic systems.
  • Research into nanoscale treatments.

Electronics

  • Smaller and faster electronic components.
  • Nanoscale sensors.
  • Improved memory and display technologies.
  • Development of advanced semiconductor devices.

Energy

  • Improved solar cells.
  • Advanced battery materials.
  • Fuel-cell technologies.
  • Energy-storage research.

Environment and Materials

  • Water purification.
  • Environmental sensors.
  • Strong and lightweight materials.
  • Protective coatings.
Possible concerns: Because nanomaterials can have unusual biological and chemical properties, their potential effects on human health and the environment must be carefully evaluated.

25.2(c) Higgs Boson

Meaning

The Higgs boson is an elementary particle associated with the Higgs field. The Higgs field is a quantum field that exists throughout space.

According to the Standard Model of particle physics, elementary particles such as the \(W\) and \(Z\) bosons and many other fundamental particles acquire mass through their interaction with the Higgs field.

Higgs boson: The Higgs boson is the quantum excitation, or particle, associated with the Higgs field.

Higgs Field and Mass

The Higgs field has a non-zero value throughout the vacuum. Fundamental particles interact with this field with different strengths. This interaction contributes to their rest mass.

The Higgs mechanism is therefore an important part of the Standard Model because it explains how the \(W\) and \(Z\) bosons acquire mass while preserving the mathematical consistency of the theory.

Discovery of the Higgs Boson

In 2012, the ATLAS and CMS experiments at the CERN Large Hadron Collider (LHC) announced the observation of a new particle with properties consistent with the Higgs boson.

The discovery provided strong experimental support for the Higgs mechanism and completed an important missing component of the Standard Model.

Exam point: The Higgs boson should not simply be described as the “particle that gives everything mass.” More accurately, it is the quantum excitation associated with the Higgs field, whose interaction with certain elementary particles contributes to their masses.

Key Points Summary

  • Earthquakes are caused mainly by sudden release of accumulated elastic energy associated with movements along faults.
  • The focus is the point inside the Earth where an earthquake originates.
  • The epicentre is the point on the Earth's surface vertically above the focus.
  • Seismic waves are classified into body waves and surface waves.
  • P-waves are longitudinal and can travel through solids, liquids and gases.
  • S-waves are transverse and can travel through solids but not liquids.
  • Rayleigh waves produce approximately elliptical particle motion and a rolling ground motion.
  • Love waves produce horizontal transverse motion.
  • In a typical seismogram, P-waves arrive before S-waves, followed by surface waves.
  • The 2015 Gorkha Earthquake generated complex seismic wave patterns recorded by seismic stations.
  • Gravitational waves are disturbances in spacetime predicted by general relativity.
  • Gravitational waves can be detected using highly sensitive laser interferometers.
  • Nanotechnology deals with the control and manipulation of matter at approximately \(1\)–\(100\,\text{nm}\) scales.
  • Nanoscale materials can have unusual electrical, optical, mechanical and chemical properties.
  • The Higgs boson is the quantum excitation associated with the Higgs field.
  • The Higgs boson was observed experimentally at the LHC in 2012 by the ATLAS and CMS collaborations.
